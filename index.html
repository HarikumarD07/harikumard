About

An engineer's discipline, brought to the boardroom.

Harikumar D brings over two decades of experience from the global automotive ecosystem, where he has led complex engineering programs, driven product transformation, and aligned diverse teams, suppliers, and stakeholders to deliver mission-critical outcomes

His professional journey spans Hindustan Aeronautics Limited, Tata Johnson Controls, Mercedes-Benz R&D, Volvo, and international assignments across Germany, France, and the USA. Starting from a strong foundation in mechanical engineering, he has evolved through roles spanning program leadership, product development, supplier governance, quality excellence, operational risk management, and enterprise transformation.

Working with global engineering organizations and customers across Europe, Japan, USA, Thailand, and India, Harikumar has developed a deep understanding of managing complexity across cultures, technologies, and business environments.

Today, he is extending this experience toward corporate governance and board leadership — bringing the same discipline that defines successful vehicle programs into the boardroom:

- Independent and objective decision-making

- Strategic risk oversight

- Technology and transformation governance

- Stakeholder accountability

- Sustainable value creation

Case Studies

Governance in practice, not just in principle.

Six situations that shaped how Harikumar leads under complexity.

Product Strategy

Managing Product Complexity

Challenge

A platform program faced conflicting requirements from engineering, regulatory and market teams simultaneously.

Approach

Built a shared prioritization framework and gave every function a transparent seat in trade-off decisions.

Outcome

Program complexity was reduced without diluting product intent, and delivery stayed on schedule.

Complexity is best managed through structure, not authority.

Supplier Governance

Supplier Risk Resolution

Challenge

A critical-path supplier showed early signs of quality and delivery risk on a high-volume component.

Approach

Escalated early, brought the supplier into joint root-cause work rather than a punitive review.

Outcome

Supply continuity was protected and the relationship emerged more resilient than before.

Early escalation is a governance tool, not a failure signal.

Cross Functional Leadership

Cross Functional Delivery

Challenge

A program spanning three countries struggled with misaligned priorities between R&D and manufacturing.

Approach

Established a single shared scorecard and a cadence that forced early, visible alignment.

Outcome

Cross-site friction dropped and the program regained a unified delivery timeline.

Alignment is a discipline that has to be designed, not assumed.

Cost Optimization

Cost Optimization

Challenge

A cost target threatened to compromise engineering integrity on a safety-relevant component.

Approach

Applied VAVE methodology to separate genuine cost opportunity from false economy.

Outcome

Savings were achieved without touching the margins that mattered for safety and quality.

Cost discipline and engineering integrity are not opposites.

Governance

Engineering Governance

Challenge

Inconsistent change-control practices were creating hidden risk across engineering releases.

Approach

Introduced a standardized governance gate with clear ownership and audit trail.

Outcome

Release risk became visible early, and accountability was traceable end to end.

Good governance makes risk visible before it becomes a crisis.

Global Collaboration

Global Collaboration

Challenge

Teams in Germany and India were working from different assumptions about program priorities.

Approach

Spent time on the ground in both locations to rebuild shared context and trust.

Outcome

A durable working rhythm formed that outlasted the original program.

Trust travels through presence, not just process.
